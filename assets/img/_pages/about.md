I am a developmental biologist studying how mouse oocytes grow and how ovarian follicles develop. In the laboratory of Allan Spradling (HHMI / Carnegie Institution for Science), I identified functionally distinct waves of ovarian follicles and showed that wave 1 follicles chiefly drive theca/interstitial cell formation and androgen production rather than contributing directly to fertility (_eLife_, 2025). My current work examines the Balbiani body, organelle selection and nuclear positioning as oocytes prepare for meiosis (_bioRxiv_, 2026).

Previously, I was a research associate at the Gurdon Institute, University of Cambridge. I received my Ph.D. in Developmental Biology from the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, where I developed haploid embryonic stem cell–based approaches for CRISPR–Cas9 genetic screening in mice (_Cell Stem Cell_, 2015).
