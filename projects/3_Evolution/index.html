Ovarian development across species

Long-term goal

While extensive studies have been performed in mice, research on other species seems to lag behind, or does it? Some would argue that it does not, since the human ovary, both fetal and adult, has been sequenced extensively (1, 2, 3, 4). Each time a new method appears, researchers, from within the reproduction field and outside it, apply it to the ovary again and re-validate what is already known (5, 6). Useful information can certainly be extracted from these data, but the field is swamped with sequencing data and relatively short of new insights.

In humans and nonhuman primates, unlike in mice, follicles begin to grow much earlier (7, 8). The granulosa cells of these follicles could induce theca cell differentiation, allowing theca cells to synthesize androgens. However, CYP19A1 (aromatase) is also expressed in pregranulosa cells (9). Are both androgens and estrogens being produced at this stage?

Direct studies of human and nonhuman primate ovaries would help us understand ourselves better.
