I am a developmental biologist studying the cellular and molecular mechanisms that govern **oocyte growth and ovarian follicle development** in the mouse. Combining genetic models, whole-mount 3D imaging and single-cell transcriptomics, I investigate how follicles differentiate and how the oocyte reorganizes its nucleus, organelles and cytoskeleton in preparation for meiosis.

In the laboratory of Allan Spradling (HHMI / Carnegie Institution for Science), I identified functionally distinct waves of ovarian follicles and showed that wave 1 follicles chiefly drive theca/interstitial cell formation and androgen production rather than contributing directly to fertility (_eLife_, 2025). My recent work shows that the oocyte **Balbiani body** reassembles in growing follicles to continue organelle selection and, upon dispersal, influences **nuclear position** (_bioRxiv_, 2026). I am now examining nuclear centralization and the dynamics of acentriolar microtubule-organizing centers (acMTOCs) during oocyte growth and meiotic preparation.

I received my Ph.D. in Developmental Biology from the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, where I developed haploid embryonic stem cell–based approaches for CRISPR–Cas9 genetic screening in mice (_Cell Stem Cell_, 2015), and subsequently worked as a research associate at the Gurdon Institute, University of Cambridge.
